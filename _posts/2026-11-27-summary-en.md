### [Who Should Decide Whether Text Is AI-Generated?](https://seangoedecke.com/who-gets-to-decide-if-something-is-written-by-ai/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 27, 00:00

**「Background」** Disputes over whether a story, manuscript, or tweet was written by AI now arise constantly, and the author notes that many anti-AI people react with hostility to detection tools such as Pangram. He argues that the real conflict is not about detector reliability or privacy, but about who holds the power to gatekeep prizes, grades, and public discourse.

**「Solution」** The author presents the skeptics&\#x27; view sympathetically: if you distrust the tech sector&\#x27;s growing social power, it is rational to reject a detector built by that same sector, and to distrust tool-shaped solutions generally as part of a broader shift from human community judgment to legible software processes. He nonetheless favors detection because the only alternative is community self-policing, which he calls terrible: people who do not use AI are poor at recognizing AI tells, often flagging long, polite human text as AI while missing obvious AI-generated prose. Ordinary investigation cannot settle authorship either, since skilled AI writing can come with plausible drafts and contemporaneous conversations, and there is no cryptographic path to attestation. His conclusion is that those who want to keep AI out of human art and culture should use the most powerful tools available, even if those tools are themselves AI, and should push for open-source detectors or government-built platforms rather than leaving the question to the community.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that the debate over AI detection is fundamentally a question of who wields the power to decide authorship, and that delegating that judgment to communities will produce more AI-written content, not less.

rss · CNBC Finance · Nov 26, 17:52

**「Background」** Deutsche Bank&\#x27;s Jim Reid framed the shift as a move from the &quot;petrodollar&quot; era, in which oil exporters recycled surpluses into U.S. Treasuries, toward a &quot;chip dollar&quot; era; the gap widened in the second quarter as oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz collapsed nearly 77% after the Iran war, per U.S. Energy Information Administration data.
